1|Assistants forget everything between conversations, or they stuff whole transcripts back into the context window. Brains do neither. So I built Alexa plus a hippocampus.
2|This is a self-hosted MCP server, spec 2025-11-25, over Streamable HTTP. A simulated Alexa plus client feeds it two weeks of ordinary life: standups, commutes, lunches, the gym.
3|Every turn opens a brand-new MCP session, just like a new Alexa conversation. Memories are written as files, not context. Important things get high salience, and those are protected forever, always stored byte-exact. When the user says good night, the agent sleeps.
4|Days later, I ask what my daughter loves. Recall is spreading activation. The cue lights up matching memories, and pulses of activation flow along association edges. Only what crosses a threshold comes back. Memories recalled together wire together, and each recall makes a memory stronger.
5|During sleep, similar episodes are consolidated into schemas, prototype memories, like the neocortex does. Hot and warm tiers are lossless; cool and cold keep the schema plus the important differences, above a measured fidelity floor. On a larger test corpus, a simulated year shrinks memory content about eight and a half times.
6|Two months later, in a fresh session, routine lunches have faded into the cool tier. Recall is a reconstruction, at least ninety percent faithful, and Alexa says so honestly. But Grandpa's checkup, high salience, comes back exactly: November 3rd, 9 a.m., ward 7B.
7|I can preview the next pass before anything is written. When it sleeps, it replays the day's memories in order, folds them into schema clusters, and condenses them. Skip ahead, and they fade to the cold tier. Nothing is ever deleted.
8|And when storage runs out? Every byte is counted: content, dictionary and index. It never silently deletes. It pushes memories to their deepest allowed tier, and if that's still not enough, Alexa asks you what to do.
9|Hippocampus: memory that sleeps, forgets gracefully, and never loses you. Open source, MIT licensed. Thanks for watching.
